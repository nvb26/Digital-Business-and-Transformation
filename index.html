The Problem

Sustainable shopping should not feel like research

A growing number of consumers want to make better choices in the supermarket, but the experience makes it nearly impossible to know what is actually sustainable in the moment.

🏷️

Confusing labels

Dozens of eco-labels, vague claims and contradictory information make it hard to compare products quickly and confidently.

⏳

Time-consuming research

Busy students and young professionals care about sustainability, but they do not have time to google every product while standing in the aisle.

🔍

Unclear sustainability impact

There is no simple, real-time tool in supermarkets that shows the true environmental impact of what we put in our basket. Greenwashing makes it even harder to trust what brands claim.

Mission & Vision

What we do today, and the future we want to build

EcoScan & Plan exists because sustainable choices should not require expertise, extra time or guesswork.

Mission

🎯 Our mission

We make sustainable grocery shopping simple, fast and trustworthy. By combining AI-driven product scanning with personalised meal planning, we help young urban consumers turn good intentions into everyday sustainable choices, without having to read every label or research every brand.

Vision

🌍 Our vision

A future where sustainable shopping is the default, not the exception. We want EcoScan & Plan to be the trusted layer between consumers and the food system: independent, transparent, and powerful enough that lower-impact products become the natural everyday choice for the next generation of shoppers.

Target Customer

Built for the next generation of conscious shoppers

Our first customer is specific: young urban consumers who already care about sustainability but lack a fast, trustworthy way to act on it in the supermarket.

Our primary segment is young urban consumers aged 18 to 35: mainly students and starting young professionals living in or around cities. They live busy, app-first lives, manage their groceries between lectures, work and social plans, and shop at regular supermarkets such as Albert Heijn, Jumbo, Lidl or Aldi rather than at specialty stores.

  • 1
    They value sustainability but lack time. Sustainability is a real driver of their choices, but reading labels and researching every brand is not realistic during a weekly grocery run.
  • 2
    They are digitally native. They trust apps for fitness, finance and dating, and expect the same level of clarity for sustainability.
  • 3
    They have limited budgets. They want better choices without paying premium prices for greenwashed products they cannot verify.
  • 4
    They are reachable. This segment is concentrated in cities and active on Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, which makes targeted, affordable user acquisition possible.
  • 5
    They are loyalty builders. Once they trust a tool, this group tends to use it weekly, share it with friends and stay with it as they move into higher-income years.

AI Meal Planner

Personal meal plans that do not repeat themselves

Our AI builds a fresh, balanced and sustainable week of meals. It adapts to your preferences, what you have already eaten and what your body needs across the week.

One AI. Three things it gets right.

The meal planner is built around three principles that make sustainable eating realistic for everyday life.

🚫 No repeat meals (14 days) ⚖️ Balanced nutrition 🌱 Lower CO₂ impact
  • ✓
    Variety, not repetition. The AI tracks a rolling 14-day meal history and avoids serving you the same dishes within that window, so you discover new recipes instead of cooking the same pasta every week.
  • ✓
    Balanced across the week. Plans aim for variety in proteins, fibre, vegetables, carbs, healthy fats, vitamins and minerals, distributed across days rather than crammed into one meal.
  • ✓
    Personalised to you. The AI learns from your sustainability preferences, dietary needs, allergies, budget, household size and shopping behaviour.
  • ✓
    Sustainable by default. Recipes prioritise seasonal produce, lower-impact proteins and ingredients with strong sustainability scores.

2 · Business Model

How EcoScan & Plan earns money

EcoScan & Plan uses a freemium model. Scanning products and seeing a sustainability score is always free, which builds an active user base. Revenue comes from premium features, ethical advertising and partnerships with organisations.

⭐

Premium subscriptions

€3.99 / month per user

Premium unlocks sustainable meal planning, smart shopping lists, personal filters (vegan, vegetarian, budget, local, allergies), scan history and sustainability progress tracking. Priced low enough for students, recurring enough to build predictable revenue.

📣

Ethical in-app advertising

€12,000 / year (Y1 estimate)

Ads are shown outside scan results and only from brands that do not compete with scanned products: green energy, public transport, sustainable banks, repair and second-hand services, educational campaigns. Food brands and supermarkets cannot advertise in scan results.

🤝

B2B pilot contracts

€25,000 per pilot

Retailers, municipalities, schools or companies can pay to test EcoScan & Plan as a sustainability awareness tool. A pilot includes testing, insights, support, reporting and possible adaptation to the partner's needs.

📊

Aggregated data insight reports

€5,000 per report

Anonymous and aggregated reports about consumer sustainability trends, product categories and shifting preferences. Individual user data is never sold and reports never contain identifiable information.

🎓

Education & company licenses

€1,500 per organisation

Affordable early access packages for schools, universities and small companies that want to use the app for workshops, education or internal sustainability programs. A low-friction way to onboard the first organisational partners.

🔄

Diversified, scalable mix

5 streams

Revenue is split across five independent channels. This reduces dependency on any single source and gives investors exposure to both consumer-paid (subscriptions) and B2B-paid (pilots, reports, licenses) value capture.

🔓

Why scans stay free, and why that protects the business

We deliberately do not charge users per scan and we do not allow brands to pay for better visibility in scan results. If companies could pay to appear as recommended alternatives, users would see the app as a marketing tool rather than an independent sustainability guide. That would damage trust, which is one of the most valuable assets of the business.

Scan results remain independent. Revenue is built around the platform, not by influencing product scores or recommendations.

Legal · Ethics · Privacy

Ethics and legal, built into the product

EcoScan & Plan handles sustainability, personalisation and AI responsibly. The product is designed around consent, privacy-by-design, secure infrastructure, transparent scoring and clear user control.

🔐

Consent first

Behavioural data, dietary preferences, allergies and personalisation are only processed with clear user consent.

🛡️

Secure by design

Encryption, MFA, access control and breach procedures are part of the operating model from day one.

🤖

Responsible AI

AI recommendations are transparent, adjustable by users and checked for bias and unreliable data.

🌿

No greenwashing

Scores are based on verifiable datasets. Brands cannot buy better scores or influence the standard ranking.

Ethical principles. EcoScan & Plan uses technology to support better consumer decisions, not to manipulate them. The user remains in control: dietary priorities can be adjusted manually, products with insufficient data are labelled clearly and AI-generated recommendations are explained transparently.

Legal position. The product follows GDPR principles, including data minimisation, privacy-by-design, transparency and user rights. For sensitive data such as allergies or dietary information, explicit consent is required. Aggregated analytics are separated from individual user profiles and identifiable personal data is never sold.

GDPR ePrivacy Directive EU AI Act transparency Unfair Commercial Practices Directive Green Claims principles CSRD-aligned transparency

🔐 Privacy & data statement

Your data is yours. EcoScan & Plan only collects what is needed to make the product work, keeps sensitive information secure and gives users direct control over their data inside the app.

  • Scan history is anonymised after 14 days and only retained for the rolling window needed by the meal algorithm
  • Separate consent choices for personalisation, score improvement analytics and B2B insights
  • Users can access, export and permanently delete their data in the app settings
  • Dietary preferences, allergies and health-related information require explicit consent
  • B2B insights are aggregated and anonymised, never traceable to individual users
  • No identifiable personal data is sold to third parties
  • Tracking cookies are only used after active opt-in
  • Data Processing Agreements are signed with all cloud and data partners

Identified risks

Risks & concrete control measures

Click any risk to see the concrete measures we take to reduce it. Each risk includes the practical controls and relevant legal basis.

EcoScan & Plan processes sensitive information such as scan behaviour, dietary preferences, allergies and location data. This requires strict consent, data minimisation and transparency.

Control measures
  • Scan history is anonymised after 14 days
  • Explicit consent for behavioural and personalisation data
  • Additional explicit consent for allergies and health-related information
  • Aggregated product-improvement analytics only where users cannot be identified
  • DPIA before every high-impact feature goes live
  • Cookie banner with active opt-in for tracking cookies
Legal basis: GDPR art. 5, 6, 9, 13, 25 and 35; ePrivacy Directive for tracking cookies.

A breach involving dietary data or scan behaviour could damage trust and trigger mandatory notification to the supervisory authority within 72 hours where legally required.

Control measures
  • AES-256 encryption at rest and TLS 1.3 encryption in transit
  • MFA for all admin and developer accounts; optional 2FA for users
  • Annual independent penetration testing before major releases
  • Role-based access control for staff and partners
  • Automated monitoring for suspicious logins and API behaviour
  • Incident response plan with internal escalation within 24 hours
Legal basis: GDPR art. 32 for technical and organisational measures; GDPR art. 33 for breach notification.

The scoring model and meal planner could unintentionally favour certain brands, diets, cuisines or expensive ingredients. That would damage trust and fairness.

Control measures
  • Scores are built on audited open databases such as Open Food Facts and Agribalyse
  • No brand-supplied ranking influence in the standard score
  • Bi-annual bias reviews for cultural, budget and diet-related bias
  • Products without reliable sustainability data receive an “insufficient data” label
  • Users can manually adjust priorities instead of relying only on automation
  • AI-generated recommendations are disclosed clearly to users
Legal basis: EU AI Act art. 50 transparency obligations; GDPR art. 22 may become relevant for significant fully automated decisions.

Incorrect sustainability scores or paid ranking influence would mislead users and undermine the core promise of the app.

Control measures
  • Public scoring methodology with data sources and weighting factors
  • CO₂, packaging, transport and certification factors are shown in the score breakdown
  • Brands cannot pay for higher scores or better standard ranking visibility
  • Sponsored content is clearly labelled when shown
  • Annual external verification by an independent sustainability organisation
  • Historical score changes are logged when methods or datasets change
Legal basis: Unfair Commercial Practices Directive 2005/29/EC and proposed EU Green Claims Directive 2023/0085.

A sustainability app should not undermine its own mission through unnecessary AI compute, storage or energy use.

Control measures
  • Use the smallest AI model suitable for each task
  • Use lightweight classification for scans and larger models only when needed for meal planning
  • Batch and cache AI calls; re-score scanned products at most once per day
  • Host in cloud regions powered largely by renewable energy
  • Publish quarterly estimated CO₂ per 1,000 API calls
  • Delete unused datasets and redundant AI logs automatically
Legal basis: No direct current obligation, but the approach aligns with broader CSRD sustainability transparency principles.

Scan behaviour, sustainability preferences and dietary patterns become highly sensitive when combined. They must never be used in ways users did not agree to.

Control measures
  • Separate opt-in or opt-out controls for each processing purpose
  • Personalisation, score improvement analytics and B2B insights are separated
  • Retail-partner data is aggregated and anonymised only
  • Users can view, export and permanently delete their data
  • Behavioural analytics are pseudonymised wherever possible
  • Recommendation logic is explained clearly to users
Legal basis: GDPR art. 17 right to erasure, art. 20 data portability and art. 21 right to object.
